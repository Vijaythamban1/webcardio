Read the call summary and takeaways

What the team described

  • POs provide interactive HTML prototypes with embedded sample data. Production implementation aims to meet the intended functionality; exact visual matching varies by request.
  • The frontend uses Angular and Angular Material. A shared component library exists and grows during feature work, without a separate team maintaining it.
  • Typography, component behavior and responsive layouts can depend on individual developers. Date-picker differences were one example discussed.
  • The earlier developer-created mock UI and approval step has changed. User-iterated prototypes are now often treated as the design reference.

Testing and unresolved questions

  • Testing exists: developer checks and code review, dedicated QA when required, internal UAT with PM/CSM involvement, and staged testing before release.
  • The team described these checks as primarily against agreed requirements and prototypes, rather than a separate usability evaluation.
  • Embedded sample data can hide live-data and network behavior. The importance of exact prototype matching differs across requests.
  • We still need PM/prototyper and relevant CSM perspectives, concrete artifacts, and the rationale behind variations.

Our takeaways

Preserve the value of fast prototyping. Investigate how user intent, existing component patterns, responsive behavior and system constraints are understood and agreed. A design system, coaching or a revised handoff are possible directions, not decisions from this call.

Discovery session · proposed structure

A proposed 60–90 minute discovery session.

Facilitated by Vijay and Sujeesh. The timeline below is the 90-minute version; we will adjust scope for a shorter session after reviewing preparation inputs. Individual writing comes before group discussion.

Aim for ~10 · maximum 15 · Mural
00–10 minSet the contextPurpose, working agreements and a quick Mural warm-up+

Explain what we are trying to learn. Invite people to speak from their own experience, describe specific events and ask questions. Show how to add a note and check everyone can participate.

Facilitator prompt

“What would make this a useful conversation for you?”

Capture

What people hope to understand, plus practical participation needs.

10–25 minHear individual perspectivesWrite quietly first, then share and clarify+

Use board area 1. Allow five minutes for individual notes, then invite contributions across roles. Ask about the user or team they serve, inputs they receive, outputs they produce and decisions they make.

Facilitator prompt

“When a small change reaches you, what do you understand, and what do you still need to find out?”

Capture

Roles, user understanding and information needs in participants’ own words.

25–55 minTrace one real change togetherRequest → prototype → decisions → implementation → feedback+

Use board areas 2 and 3. Let the example owner give a short recap, then invite PM, engineering and QA to add their part. Check the order, handoffs and decision points together. Use artifacts to clarify different recollections.

Facilitator prompt

“At this point, what did you know, what did you assume, and what had been agreed with the user?”

Capture

Who did what, available context, decisions, expectation changes and the outcome. Mark unknown steps.

55–80 minExplore strengths and frictionUnderstand the reasons behind the way people work+

Use board areas 4 and 5. Begin with what helped. Explore where work became harder and what constraints or tradeoffs shaped it. Ask whether each issue was specific to this case or has other examples. Keep explanations as hypotheses until checked.

Facilitator prompt

“What made this difficult? What were you trying to protect or achieve? What was the consequence?”

Capture

Specific strengths, examples of friction, competing interpretations and evidence still needed.

80–90 minCheck what we heard and closeConfirm themes, retain disagreements and agree follow-up+

Use board area 6. Read back the emerging picture. Ask for corrections and invite anything people could not raise. Select two or three areas to examine further, then name follow-up owners and agree how findings will be reviewed.

Facilitator prompt

“What have we misunderstood? What should we look at more closely before shaping the next workshop?”

Capture

Priorities for further investigation, unresolved questions, owners and a readout plan.

Outputs we can use

  • Current process map grounded in a recent example.
  • Role perspectives and information needs.
  • Strengths, friction and different explanations.
  • Follow-up questions and potential workshop priorities.
